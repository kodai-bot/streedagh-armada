# Finds study: guns, rudder and anchor (plan)

Status: research plan, noted 2026-11-07. Not started.

A study of the material recovered from the Streedagh wrecks, starting with La Juliana's guns.

## The guns

The guns can be traced to their foundries, dates and patrons.

### What the local sources say

**Ridella et al. 2016**, "The Cadiz-Delta II wreck: the San Giorgio, a Genoese merchantman sunk by Francis Drake in 1587", *Archeologia Postmedievale* 20 (`sources/The_Cadiz-Delta_II_wreck_the_San_Giorgio.pdf`):

- **One founder links the two wrecks.**
  - Juliana's recovered bronze guns were cast in Genoa in **1570**, the year she was launched, by **Dorino II Gioardi**. They carry his **D** on the touchhole, Type C muzzle mouldings, and a saint on the reinforce (pp. 27, 40).
  - On the San Giorgio wreck at Cádiz:
    - the heaviest gun carries the G of **Gregorio II Gioardi**, Dorino's cousin and business partner (from 1569);
    - one saker (CNN 5, 14.04 cantara, 670 kg) is attributed to Dorino himself, though the authors call it **only a hypothesis** (pp. 36–38).
- **His surviving guns are counted at 13 (tab. 1, p. 41).** Dorino's surviving guns are the most numerous from any single Genoese founder of the 16th century: "his work is represented by **13 pieces**".
  - **9 of the 13 are from La Juliana**: 3 recovered in 1985 (the St. Severo heavy saker and the two petrieri, McElvogue 2002) and 6 in 2015 (Moore et al. 2015).
  - The other 4 are:
    - a bastard demi-culverin off Fornells, Menorca;
    - a heavy saker cast for the walls of Palermo (1576; now Museo San Telmo, San Sebastián);
    - two pieces from the San Juan / Parissona grossa wreck off Sciacca, Sicily.
  - **§29 of the reconstruction is this table's 9 Juliana rows**, which is why it itemises 9, not 12. The other 3 of Juliana's 12 recovered guns are not Dorino's, or can't yet be attributed. The text says "**eight** of these bronze guns bearing a D … were recovered … in 1985 and 2015" (p. 27). That presumably counts the guns with a legible D, since the two petrieri have no weight mark.
- **The buyer.** In summer 1569 Dorino priced his finished cannon at the request of Francisco Reul **from Barcelona** (ASGe, NA, f. 1873, 26.VIII.1569; p. 40). That looks like the commissioning of Juliana's battery.
- **Ragusa to Barcelona.** Genoese founders sold petrieri (anti-boarding stone-throwers) "in large amounts … for ship-owners not only from Genoa but also from Spain and **Ragusa** (present-day Dubrovnik). One example is the Catalan merchantman … La Juliana" (p. 29).
  - Across the Streedagh trio, the ships came from Venice, Barcelona and Ragusa.
  - The same Genoese trade is the thread to test across all three.
- **Calibration.** The cantaro is fixed at 47.65 kg because the San Giorgio and Juliana guns share founders. That is the value used in §29.
- **Dates to reconcile.**
  - The paper dates Juliana's loss to 26 September 1588 (citing Birch 1998). The project uses 21 September Gregorian (11 September Julian).
  - It also gives Juliana as 850 metric tons, against the project's 860 toneladas.

**Birch & McElvogue 1999**, "La Lavia, La Juliana and the Santa Maria de Vison", *IJNA* 28.3 (`sources/ArmadaWrecksStreedagh.pdf`), for Site 1 (Juliana):

- **Three iron anchors and three bronze guns** lay on top of the concretion. The three guns were lifted and conserved by the National Museum.
- Spanish archives match three of the bronze guns seen on the seabed to an inventory of the original armament.
- Five wooden spoked wheels were recorded.
- The **rudder** was uncovered in 1985, "overlying another bronze gun with lifting dolphins".
- A wrought-iron anchor marks the lowest point of the site.

The same paper records finds at the other two sites:

- **Site 2 (Visón):** a bronze breech-loading esmeril with the arms of Philip II. It is not a Juliana gun, and could be how some counts reach 13.
- **Site 3 (Lavia):** two gun carriages with their guns, one with a bronze medio sacre still in place.

**Not yet read for this:** Colin Martin's thesis (`sources/ColinMartinPhDThesis.pdf`, 60 MB), and McElvogue 1998 on a 16th-century rudder assemblage (*IJNA* 27.1), a comparison for the Streedagh rudder.

## Other finds to include

- **The rudder.** Recorded in the wreck; it shifted after long burial (the 2015 exposure). One early
  report also notes a bronze gun lying under the rudder (superseded/v1_tonnage_model/streedagh_manifest_notes.md).
- **An anchor about 17 feet long, recently uncovered.** Reported to the project; not yet in the project
  text. Details and source still to add.
- Already noted in the project: carriage wheels from the siege train, a bronze cauldron, and the anchors
  recorded in the diver survey.

## What the study would do

1. **Inventory.** One table of every recovered object, recording:
   - type and mass;
   - founder, date and marks;
   - recovery year and position;
   - source.
2. **Provenance.** Trace foundry, founder, saint and patron for each gun across the three ships.
3. **Stowage and stations.** Run the §29.1 test: join gun type and mass to recovery position and seabed
   context, to see whether the lighter pieces sit apart from the heavy ones. That would be consistent with
   guns mounted high in the stepped castles (see [the capacity script](../2_reconstruction/stepped_castle_capacity.py)
   and the [Standing room](https://kodai-bot.github.io/streedagh/ships/) page).
4. **Rudder and anchor.**
   - The rudder's size can serve as an independent check on the reconstructed hull, which is about
     30.5 m long and 6.4 m deep.
   - The anchor's size can be compared with the anchoring evidence and with the ship's rating.

The three ships at Streedagh are war graves; this works only from published recoveries and reports.
