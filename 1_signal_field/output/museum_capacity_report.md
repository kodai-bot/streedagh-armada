# Museum and conservation capacity: brackets

Generated by `museum_capacity.py` from the seabed mass budget's own draws (100,000; budget seed 20261107, capacity seed 20261109). Median [5th–95th percentile].

**What this is.** If a given fraction of the material the budget puts on the seabed survives and is raised, what must a facility hold and treat? The fraction is a planning bracket, not an estimate, and it is applied to every class alike (in reality bronze survives better than timber). The answers are orders of magnitude for infrastructure planning, to be replaced by survey and by a conservator's specification.

The basis on the seabed (all three ships): 826 [623–1,084] t in all, of which hull timber 331 [189–541] t, iron 113 [76–162] t, ballast 215 [132–299] t, cargo 69 [13–142] t. Guns: 75 documented, 12 already raised.

## Reading it

- **Timber sets the scale.** Even at an eighth, the hull timber needs a tank of about 100 m³ for a decade or more. If everything survives, the PEG tank comes out at about 800 m³, the size of the tank the Bremen cog needed (a check, not an input: the tank factor was not fitted to it). Freeze-drying shortens the time but needs a chamber the size of Texas A&M's for years.
- **The guns are the smallest volume and the best known.** The National Museum's own process for the 2015 guns (fresh-water tanks, then mechanical cleaning, then de-ionised washes) is the local baseline.
- **Iron is the hidden load.** Concretions hide their contents until X-rayed and opened, and each batch ties up tanks for years.
- **Not counted:** small finds and organics beyond the hull (casks, the carriage wheels, personal possessions), staff, money, and display design. Human remains are a matter of protocol, not capacity: the site is a war grave.

## Assumptions

| Prior | values | basis |
|---|---|---|
| oak_service_density | uniform (0.75, 1.0) | t/m3 of hull timber in service, seasoned to wet; turns the budget's timber mass into volume. HYPOTHESIS |
| basic_density_waterlogged | uniform (0.25, 0.5) | g/cm3 dry matter per wet volume after four centuries; with dry-matter density 1.5 g/cm3 it gives the wet bulk density. HYPOTHESIS |
| peg_tank_factor | uniform (1.5, 3.0) | tank volume per m3 of dismantled timber (packing, solution, access). The Bremen cog needed an 800 m3 tank for one ship. HYPOTHESIS |
| peg_years | uniform (10.0, 20.0) | years of PEG treatment: Vasa 17 (spray), Mary Rose about 19 (spray), Bremen cog about 18-20 (immersion); dismantled timbers may go faster |
| freeze_load_frac | uniform (0.3, 0.6) | share of a freeze-dryer chamber filled per load. HYPOTHESIS |
| freeze_cycle_months | uniform (4.0, 6.0) | months per freeze-drying load (La Belle, Texas A&M) |
| concretion_per_t_iron | uniform (1.5, 3.0) | tonnes of concretion (iron, corrosion products, sand, shell, trapped shot) per tonne of original iron. HYPOTHESIS |
| concretion_density | uniform (1.8, 2.5) | t/m3. HYPOTHESIS |
| electrolysis_tank_factor | uniform (4.0, 10.0) | tank volume per m3 of concreted iron (solution around each object). HYPOTHESIS |
| iron_treatment_years | uniform (2.0, 5.0) | years of desalination or electrolytic reduction per batch: about four years for cannon at the QAR lab; small finds faster |
| timber_stack_height_m | uniform (0.4, 0.8) | effective height of timber in wet storage or tanks per m2 of floor. HYPOTHESIS |
| gun_floor_m2 | uniform (6.0, 12.0) | m2 of floor per gun in storage or display, with access and its carriage or cradle. HYPOTHESIS |
