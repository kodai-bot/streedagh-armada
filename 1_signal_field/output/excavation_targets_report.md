## Reading it

- **Site 1 first, for monitoring and rescue.** It holds the most material and is the most exposed: the only site with dated exposure episodes (1985, 2015) and the one the divers call 'the most dynamic'. Timber is what it loses fastest. Be clear what is new here: the ranking mostly restates the record, because the exposure rates were set from those same descriptions. What the model adds is the scale (tonnes a decade, with an interval) and which assumption matters most (Site 1's exposure rate, then how much of a site one storm uncovers).
- **Site 3 holds almost as much and loses least.** Its sand cover is protection. It is the place to leave alone and to monitor, not to open, unless survey shows the cover thinning.
- **Site 2 is the unknown.** A compact, high-intensity spread with only isolated finds: one dated exposure record there would change its rank more than any other single fact.
- **Within Site 1** positions are not held, so targets stay qualitative: the site assessment's predicted buried heavy tier seaward of the ballast and concretion (Part III), and the break-up prediction that castle guns lie inshore of the keel line (site record). The 1985 account places the articulated structure, concretions, anchors and guns about 20 m seaward of the wheels.
- **What would sharpen it most:** dated sand-level or exposure records for each site (the NMS monitoring, if released), and the recent surveys' positions.
