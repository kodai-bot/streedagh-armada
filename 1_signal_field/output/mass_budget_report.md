## Not in this layer

Hull timber, ballast, anchors and ground tackle, rig, provisions and water, the soldiers' personal arms, and cargo (Juliana's siege-carriage wheels): layer 2, below.

The people are not counted as mass. The muster lists 976 soldiers and seamen on the three ships in May; Cuéllar says more than a thousand drowned, and the Lord Deputy reported 1,100 bodies counted along the strand (Martin p. 111). The site is a war grave.

# Layer 2: the whole ship

**To the seabed on the wreck day, the three ships together: 826.4 [622.6–1084.3] t.** Of 1413.2 [1103.9–1800.6] t aboard (people excluded), the rest, 576.6 [387.7–845.1] t, was mostly timber, rig and casks that broke away and drove onto the strand.

How it is built. The measured dimensions (Lavia, Visón) or the v0.7 envelope (Juliana) give, through a hull-form model, the displacement fully laden. The hull itself (lightship) is a share of that. On the wreck day the ship carried the lightship plus named loads, each from a source: layer 1's ordnance; ballast scaled from the Rosa's excavated mound (Martin p. 150); what was left of the stores, on Medina Sidonia's ration scale (pp. 349-350); the soldiers' arms; siege cargo (Valencera's issue list, pp. 115-116; Juliana's five carriage wheels). The draught on the day is then derived, not assumed. Each part is split into what stayed on the seabed and what floated off.

The consistency check is the May column. Put the May loading back aboard (six months' rations, Martin p. 349; munitions as mustered) and the ships come out close to the laden displacement that the hull model gives from their dimensions. The rations and the hull were not fitted to each other, so this is a real test of the priors, though not an independent one (both use lightship_frac). On the wreck day, with the stores mostly gone, they ride about a metre and a half above that line: light, with tall castles, the windage condition of v0.7 §9 near a lee shore.

So the seabed figure is mostly a question about the hull: how much of it stayed (timber_retained) and how heavy it was (lightship_frac), then the ballast. Each has a field test:

- **Timber extent and depth** at Sites 1-3 (probing, sub-bottom profiling): how much lower hull lies under the ballast.
- **The ballast mounds** themselves, measured as Martin measured the Rosa's (area x depth x packing).
- **The Streedagh rudder's length**, read as Martin read the Tobermory rudder (p. 217): the laden draught.
- **Issue lists for Juliana, Lavia and Visón** like the Valencera's (AGS), for the cargo.
